# Investment Style

Updated: 2026-05-16

## Investor Identity

I am primarily a value-oriented buy-and-hold investor. I look for high-quality businesses when the market is underestimating them, usually because investors are focused too much on a near-term fear, narrative risk, or temporary uncertainty.

My default preference is to buy durable companies at cheap or reasonable prices and hold them while the thesis plays out.

## Core Philosophy

- Buy businesses that look cheaper than their long-term quality deserves.
- Prefer companies with durable moats, strong cash flow, and multiple ways to keep growing.
- Look for cases where the market overreacts to disruption risk.
- Hold positions when the thesis is intact instead of trading around short-term noise.
- Require a clear reason why the market's concern may be overstated.

## Current Pattern

My current investing pattern is to find large, high-quality companies that still look undervalued relative to their future earnings power.

Examples:

- GOOGL: bought when the P/E was around the low 20s because the market appeared too worried that AI would disrupt Search. My thesis was that Google had a credible AI strategy: integrate AI into existing products, create new AI products such as NotebookLM, and bundle AI into products it can monetize.
- MSFT: owned because the market may still underprice the durability of Azure, Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot, and enterprise AI distribution.
- META: owned because the market may overfocus on AI capex while underestimating the cash-generating ad business, AI ad optimization, and future consumer AI products such as AI glasses.

## Return Objective

My objective is to outperform the S&P 500 over time.

If the current strategy is not enough to beat the S&P 500, future analysis should recommend strategy adjustments rather than only confirming my existing bias.

## Known Biases And Risks

- I may avoid expensive-looking AI hardware stocks even when the growth, demand cycle, and earnings revisions justify the valuation.
- I may miss momentum or high-growth opportunities because they do not initially look cheap on simple P/E or P/S metrics.
- I may overweight mega-cap quality stocks because they feel safer and easier to understand.
- I may hold undervalued stocks too long if the thesis weakens slowly.
- I may need a better framework for when a premium valuation is acceptable.

## Strategy Questions To Keep Asking

- Is this stock genuinely undervalued, or just lower quality than the market leaders?
- Is the market fear temporary, or is the business model actually being impaired?
- Does the company have enough growth to outperform the index from today's price?
- Is a higher-multiple stock actually cheaper on future earnings power because growth is underestimated?
- What am I missing by staying too strict on valuation?
- Would this idea improve my chance of outperforming the S&P 500, or only feel comfortable?

## Agent Guidance

When analyzing investments for me:

- Start with valuation, but do not stop at valuation.
- Compare the idea against the S&P 500 opportunity cost.
- Identify whether the thesis is value, growth, quality, turnaround, or momentum.
- Challenge me when my value bias may cause missed upside.
- Recommend strategy changes when my current approach is unlikely to outperform.
- Separate cheap stocks from mispriced compounders.
- Make clear what would change the conclusion.

## Strategy Evolution

Future recommendations can suggest new strategy modules, including:

- Quality growth at a fair price.
- Growth-adjusted valuation using PEG, revenue growth, margin expansion, and free cash flow growth.
- AI infrastructure and hardware cycle exposure when earnings power is compounding faster than valuation risk.
- Portfolio barbell: undervalued compounders plus a smaller basket of higher-growth leaders.
- S&P 500 benchmark checks before adding or holding a position.
